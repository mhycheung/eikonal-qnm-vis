Eikonal quasinormal modes at the Schwarzschild light ring

polarization:

Each line is a Schwarzschild null geodesic ($M=1$), integrated exactly. The rays start near the equatorial light ring $r=3M$, parallel to it, on a small disk of radius $w_0$. The closed loops are the bundle's cross-section on a slice of constant Schwarzschild time $t$.

Radial direction: unstable. Offsets grow as $\delta r\propto e^{\lambda t}$, with $\lambda=\Omega=1/(3\sqrt3 M)$: a factor $e^{2\pi}\approx 535$ per orbit. Rays outside the ring escape; rays inside fall towards the horizon (they freeze there in Schwarzschild $t$).

Out-of-plane direction: neutral. A neighbouring ray lies on a tilted great circle of the same sphere. The bundle therefore focuses to a radial line a quarter orbit after it starts, and every half orbit after that.

Repeating bundles. Near the ring the flow is linear and symmetric under rotation, so the bundle at $t+T$ is the bundle at $t$, rotated by $\Omega T$ and stretched by $e^{\lambda T}$. So, after each interval $T$ a fresh copy of the original bundle starts, while the older one keeps growing and fades.

Fading = damping. The cross-section area grows as $e^{\lambda t}$. Flux conservation then gives an amplitude $\propto e^{-\lambda t/2}$: the decay rate $\lvert\mathrm{Im}\,\omega\rvert=\lambda/2$ of the fundamental mode.

Rays start on a great circle of the photon sphere $r=3M$ (the equator). All of them have orbital planes tilted by $\iota$ from the equatorial plane. Each ray has radial neighbours at $r=3M\pm\epsilon$ (orange outside, blue inside), which peel away exponentially. Loops join the heads of the rays.

$\iota=90^\circ$: every ray heads north along a meridian. The ring shrinks to a point at the north pole, comes back out through the equator, focuses at the south pole, and repeats. This is the ray picture of an $m=0$ mode; the caustics at the poles match the peaks of $Y_{\ell 0}$ there.

In general a mode $(\ell,m)$ corresponds to planes with $\cos\iota = m/(\ell+\tfrac12)$. Its rays fill the band of latitudes $\lvert\pi/2-\theta\rvert\le\iota$, with caustics at its edges. $\iota=0$ is the equatorial $\ell=m$ mode.

The rays are the same. At leading eikonal order, scalar, electromagnetic and gravitational waves follow the same null geodesics, and the quasinormal-mode frequencies agree: $\omega\approx\Omega(\ell+\tfrac12)-i\lambda(n+\tfrac12)$. Spin-dependent deviations from the geodesics (the gravitational spin Hall effect) enter at first order in wavelength$/M$, roughly $1/\ell$.

The strain is transverse to the ray. In geometric optics $h_{ab}=\mathcal{A}\,e_{ab}\,e^{i\Psi}$ with $e_{ab}k^b=0$, and the polarization tensor is parallel-transported along the ray. The condition for this is wavelength $\ll M$, i.e. $\ell\gg1$; distance from the hole does not enter. For a planar orbit, the reflection symmetry keeps one polarization axis along $\hat\theta$, normal to the orbital plane, and the other along $\hat r$. So the axes do not rotate around the orbit.

What is drawn. Rings of free test particles held by static observers on the light ring, in the plane $(\hat r,\hat\theta)$ perpendicular to the propagation direction $\hat\phi$. They are deformed by $h_{+}$, $h_{\times}$ of the mode $e^{i(m\phi-\omega t)}$; magenta = stretched along $\hat r$, green = stretched along $\hat\theta$ (for $\times$, the two diagonals). The pattern moves around the ring at the light-ring angular speed. The envelope across latitude follows $\lvert Y_{\ell\ell}\rvert\propto\sin^{\ell}\theta$. The amplitude is drawn without the slow decay $e^{-\lambda t/2}$. Cyan: the ray bundle. Its own change of shape (radial stretching, out-of-plane focusing) is background optics, not the wave's strain.

Finite $\ell$. For $\ell=2$, $M\omega=0.374-0.089i$, the reduced wavelength is $\approx2.7M$. $r=3M$ is then at the outer edge of the near zone (Nichols et al. 2012, arXiv:1208.3038). The tidal field there has longitudinal parts of order one, and this ray picture is only qualitative.
